## Legislation, Standards and Cybersecurity Management in Finnish Healthcare

Today, I also explored how legislation, international standards, and cybersecurity management frameworks guide cybersecurity in Finnish health and social care organizations.

Several European and Finnish regulations are particularly relevant to the healthcare sector.

The **General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)** establishes requirements for the processing and protection of personal data, including sensitive health information.

The **NIS2 Directive** aims to strengthen cybersecurity across critical sectors, including healthcare. In Finland, its key requirements have been implemented through the **Cybersecurity Act (124/2025)**. Organizations within the scope of this legislation must address cybersecurity risk management, incident reporting, and management responsibilities.

In addition, the Finnish **Act on the Processing of Client Data in Healthcare and Social Welfare (703/2023)** regulates the processing of patient and client information and establishes requirements related to information security.

The **Medical Device Regulation (MDR)** is also relevant because medical devices and related software must meet applicable safety and performance requirements, including cybersecurity considerations.

Alongside legislation, international standards support systematic cybersecurity management. **ISO/IEC 27001** defines requirements for an Information Security Management System (ISMS), while **ISO/IEC 27002** provides guidance on information security controls.

Traficom's **Cybermeter (Kybermittari)** supports organizations in assessing their cybersecurity maturity and identifying areas for improvement. The tool is largely based on the Cybersecurity Capability Maturity Model (C2M2).

### Reflection

I learned that legislation, standards, and cybersecurity assessment tools have different but complementary roles.

Legislation establishes mandatory requirements for organizations within its scope, while standards and assessment frameworks provide structured approaches to managing and improving cybersecurity.

In healthcare, these requirements are particularly important because cybersecurity incidents may affect not only information systems and sensitive data but also patient safety and continuity of care.

**My key takeaway is that cybersecurity in healthcare is both a regulatory responsibility and an essential part of organizational risk management and patient safety.**

### Reflection

An important learning point was understanding the differences between legislation, standards, and management frameworks.

Legislation establishes legal obligations, while standards and assessment tools provide methods for implementing, evaluating, and improving cybersecurity practices.

Not all requirements apply to every healthcare organization in the same way. Their applicability depends on the organization's activities, role, and legal status.

I also realized that cybersecurity preparedness cannot be assessed solely from a technical perspective. In healthcare, organizational structures, leadership, workforce capabilities, and patient safety must be considered together.
