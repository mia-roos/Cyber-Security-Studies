
# H2 – Learning Diary: Cybersecurity Presentations

**Date:** 28 October 2026  
**Course:** Cyber Security – Haaga-Helia University of Applied Sciences

## 1. My Presentation and Reflection

**Topic:** Cybersecurity in Finnish Social and Healthcare Services: Current Threats, Patient Safety and Preparedness

My presentation focused on cybersecurity in Finnish social and healthcare services, particularly its connection to patient safety, trust and organisational preparedness.

Overall, I am satisfied with my presentation and the feedback I received. The overall evaluation was **Good**, and the peer reviews were predominantly very positive.

### Peer Feedback

The feedback highlighted several strengths:

- Clear structure and understandable content.
- A strong connection between cybersecurity and patient safety.
- Practical experience and a healthcare professional's perspective.
- The importance of trust, staff awareness and organisational preparedness.
- Good presentation skills, English language skills and interaction with the audience.

Several reviewers mentioned that the presentation helped them understand cybersecurity as more than a technical issue.

One reviewer described the topic as very broad and appreciated that I managed to provide a foundation for understanding cybersecurity in healthcare. This made me reflect on the balance between providing an overview and exploring a specific issue in greater depth.

The feedback also included constructive suggestions, such as providing more concrete examples, practical solutions and technical details, as well as reducing repetition.

### My Own Reflection

Time management was one of my main challenges. I had to speed up towards the end of the presentation, which affected the flow.

Presenting in English was also challenging at times, as I occasionally struggled to find the right words.

**My main learning point is the importance of a clearly defined focus and carefully selected key messages.**

In future presentations, I want to:

- Narrow the topic and define the main message more precisely.
- Prioritise the most relevant information.
- Include more concrete examples and practical solutions.
- Practise my presentation and timing, especially in English.

I am pleased that my central message was understood: **cybersecurity in healthcare is fundamentally connected to patient safety and trust, not just technology.**

## 2. Learning from Other Presentations

### Cybersecurity Leadership and the Role of a CISO

One presentation explored the first months of a Chief Information Security Officer (CISO) in an organisation.

My most important learning point was that effective cybersecurity leadership requires a thorough understanding of the organisation, its business, history, structure, critical operations and risks.

Several ideas stood out:

- Understand the organisation before introducing new security programmes.
- Listen to employees and stakeholders.
- Compare existing security documentation with operational reality.
- Clarify who owns and manages cybersecurity risks.
- Integrate security responsibilities into everyday organisational activities.
- Focus on implementation rather than creating perfect plans that are never put into practice.
- Document improvements and achievements over time.
- Understand the financial aspects of cybersecurity and communicate them to management.

I particularly appreciated the idea that **an implemented solution that is 80% complete may be more valuable than a perfect plan that is never implemented.**

This made me reflect on healthcare organisations, where policies and guidelines do not necessarily guarantee that practices are implemented consistently.

Cybersecurity requires leadership, organisational commitment and continuous improvement.

### PHP Vulnerabilities and Web Application Security

Another presentation explored PHP vulnerabilities and how attackers may exploit weaknesses in web applications.

PHP was a relatively unfamiliar technology to me, so the presentation introduced several new concepts.

I learned about:

- PHP as a server-side scripting language used in web applications.
- How browsers, web servers and PHP interact when processing requests.
- Memory allocation and memory limits.
- How vulnerabilities in memory handling may be exploited.
- The Common Vulnerability Scoring System (CVSS), which is used to assess vulnerability severity.
- The importance of software updates and vulnerability management.

The presenter used a restaurant analogy to explain a technically complex vulnerability. This helped me understand the basic idea of how an attacker might exploit a weakness in a system.

One question I found particularly interesting was:

**How does an attacker determine the precise request needed to exploit a vulnerability?**

The presentation demonstrated how technically complex cybersecurity incidents can be and how important it is to understand the underlying technology.

I would like to learn more about PHP, web application architecture and how organisations identify and respond to these types of vulnerabilities.

## 4. Key Takeaways

This lesson demonstrated how cybersecurity combines technical knowledge, organisational leadership and human factors.

The presentations approached cybersecurity from different perspectives, but they shared an important message: **effective cybersecurity requires understanding both technology and the environment in which it operates.**

For me, the most important learning points were:

- Cybersecurity responsibilities must be integrated into organisational leadership and everyday operations.
- Security policies are only useful when they are implemented in practice.
- Technical vulnerabilities can have consequences beyond the affected systems.
- Communication and practical examples are essential when explaining cybersecurity to different audiences.
- A focused presentation with clear key messages is often more effective than attempting to cover an entire broad topic.

I am satisfied with my progress and the feedback I received. The experience also strengthened my motivation to continue learning about cybersecurity, particularly its connections to healthcare, organisational resilience and patient safety.
