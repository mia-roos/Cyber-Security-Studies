### Threat Modeling in Healthcare

I created a fictional 24-hour residential care unit for older adults in Helsinki and identified its most important assets: sensitive health information, continuity of care, organizational reputation and financial resources.

I examined two cybersecurity risks:

1. **Phishing:** An attacker obtains a healthcare professional's login credentials and potentially gains unauthorised access to confidential health information.
2. **Ransomware:** An attack prevents healthcare professionals from accessing electronic health records for three days, potentially disrupting medication administration and other essential care activities.

I practised estimating the likelihood and financial impact of these risks and calculating their expected annual losses.

One important insight was that financial risk calculations have limitations in healthcare. The financial costs of a cyberattack do not fully reflect its potential consequences for patient safety.

### Risk Management and Cyber Resilience

I also learned more about the META risk treatment strategies: Mitigate, Eliminate, Transfer and Accept.

For the phishing risk, I selected **Mitigate**, focusing on staff cybersecurity training and multi-factor authentication (MFA).

Our discussion about ransomware helped me understand the difference between backups and business continuity planning. Backups support data recovery, whereas business continuity planning helps ensure that essential healthcare services can continue during a system disruption.

I reflected on how medication administration could be managed if electronic health records were unavailable. Paper medication lists may provide an alternative, but they are not always available or up to date. Healthcare professionals may need to consult physicians and other reliable sources to verify medication information.

This highlighted the importance of preparedness, clear procedures and collaboration across the entire organisation.

### Personal Reflection

As a healthcare professional and educator, I find the connection between cybersecurity and patient safety particularly important.

Today's work helped me understand that cybersecurity is not only a technical responsibility. It requires organisational leadership, staff awareness, multidisciplinary collaboration and continuous risk management.

I also realised that identifying a risk is only the first step. Organizations must be prepared to respond to incidents, maintain essential services and recover safely.

### Take-Home Message

**Cybersecurity in healthcare is not only about protecting data and information systems. It is also about protecting patients, supporting healthcare professionals and ensuring continuity of care.**

Effective cybersecurity requires both technical safeguards and organisational preparedness.
