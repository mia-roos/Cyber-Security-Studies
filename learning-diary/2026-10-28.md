
# Learning Diary – 28 October 2026

## Finalising My Cybersecurity Presentation

Today, I finalised my presentation, *Cybersecurity in Finnish Social and Healthcare Services*, for the Cyber Security course at Haaga-Helia University of Applied Sciences.

My main objective was to create a clear, evidence-based presentation that connects cybersecurity with patient safety, trust, and the everyday realities of healthcare.

I reviewed and refined the presentation content, visual design, references, and speaker notes. I also worked on communicating complex cybersecurity concepts in a way that would be understandable to an audience without extensive technical knowledge.

## What Did I Learn?

### 1. Cybersecurity threats have practical consequences for patient care

I explored how phishing, ransomware, and security vulnerabilities can affect healthcare organisations. Cyber incidents may make patient records unavailable, delay treatments, disrupt clinical workflows, and increase patient safety risks.

This strengthened my understanding that cybersecurity is not only about protecting digital systems. It is also about maintaining safe and continuous patient care.

### 2. Cybersecurity is a sociotechnical challenge

I examined research emphasising the interconnected roles of people, organisations, and technology.

The literature highlights the importance of staff competence, organisational leadership, security culture, and technical safeguards. I realised that cybersecurity preparedness cannot be assessed solely from a technical perspective.

### 3. Legislation, standards, and management frameworks have different roles

I reviewed key regulations affecting Finnish healthcare, including:

- General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)
- NIS2 Directive
- Finnish Cybersecurity Act
- Client Data Act
- Medical Device Regulation (MDR)

I also explored cybersecurity standards and frameworks:

- ISO/IEC 27001 and ISO/IEC 27002
- NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) 2.0
- Traficom's Cybermeter (Kybermittari)

An important learning point was understanding that **legislation establishes legal obligations, while standards and assessment tools support systematic cybersecurity management and improvement.**

### 4. Cybersecurity awareness is an essential concept

I explored the meaning of *cybersecurity awareness* and its relevance to healthcare professionals.

Cybersecurity awareness involves recognising cyber risks, understanding their possible consequences, and knowing how everyday actions can help prevent security incidents.

I also learned to distinguish awareness from practical competence. Recognising a risk is important, but healthcare professionals must also know how to respond appropriately.

### 5. Expert perspectives complement scientific literature

I incorporated insights from a Finnish healthcare sector expert interview conducted on 26 October 2026.

The interview highlighted practical challenges related to:

- Workload and staff turnover
- Multilingual working environments
- Cybersecurity training and competence
- Artificial intelligence and data protection

These perspectives helped me connect theoretical knowledge with the realities of healthcare work.

However, I also recognised the importance of distinguishing an individual expert's observations from findings supported by scientific research.

## Reflection

Preparing this presentation was a valuable learning experience because it required me to combine scientific research, legislation, cybersecurity frameworks, and practical healthcare perspectives.

My professional background in nursing, healthcare leadership, education, and research influenced how I approached the topic.

I was particularly interested in understanding what cybersecurity means for healthcare professionals who depend on digital systems but may have limited influence over their technical security.

I also reflected on the importance of **trust**. Patients are often in vulnerable situations, and they need to trust that healthcare organisations can protect their information and provide safe care, including during digital disruptions.

I used AI tools to support the development of the presentation, including language refinement, visual design, and illustrative images. I reviewed the content and sources and marked AI-generated imagery appropriately.

## Key Takeaway

**Cybersecurity in healthcare is not simply an IT responsibility. It requires coordinated leadership, competent professionals, secure technologies, and organisational preparedness to protect patient safety and maintain continuity of care.**

Finalising the presentation also helped me identify a topic I would like to explore further:

**How can healthcare organisations systematically assess and strengthen cybersecurity awareness and preparedness among healthcare professionals?**
